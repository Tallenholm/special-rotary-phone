# Phase-0 v0.8 Governance Refinements — Validation, Portfolio Reporting, and LLM Use

**Date:** August 14, 2026  
**Status:** Accepted v0.8 remediation rule set

These rules refine three recommendations raised during the v0.7 adversarial audit. They preserve strict anti-leakage controls without replacing useful research diagnostics with overly absolute constraints.

## 1. CPCV is a development diagnostic, not a substitute for forward evidence

Combinatorial Purged Cross-Validation (CPCV) is a historical development and diagnostic tool. It is useful for evaluating model stability, selection bias, path dependence, and the Probability of Backtest Overfitting (PBO), but it cannot create genuinely unseen market regimes, future regulatory structures, or future liquidity states.

### Validation hierarchy

1. **Development / architecture layer**
   - chronological training and development splits;
   - nested validation where tuning occurs;
   - purging and embargo where labels overlap;
   - CPCV / PBO / Deflated Sharpe or related diagnostics where appropriate;
   - no development diagnostic is treated as fresh forward evidence.

2. **Sealed chronological confirmation layer**
   - untouched chronological windows remain one-use confirmatory evidence for a research generation;
   - once inspected, a sealed window is recorded as exposed history;
   - CPCV does not reset or erase that exposure.

3. **Prospective forward layer**
   - live paper / forward observations collected after the strategy and rules are frozen;
   - prospective evidence outranks recycled historical evidence when determining whether a signal survives into deployment research.

### Governing rule

**CPCV may reduce historical selection risk and conserve scarce data during development, but it must never be described as a replacement for genuinely unseen chronological or prospective evidence.**

## 2. Long-short anomaly tests require dual portfolio reporting

Dollar-neutral spreads remain valuable because they preserve comparability with the academic literature and show what the raw unconstrained sort actually experiences. However, dollar neutrality does not imply beta neutrality or volatility neutrality.

### Mandatory reporting

Every long-short anomaly/factor experiment must report both portfolio views by default:

1. **Raw dollar-neutral spread**
   - conventional `1.0 long / 1.0 short` gross-leg construction or the closest predeclared academic replication;
   - long and short legs reported separately;
   - realized market beta, sector exposure, volatility, turnover, and financing shown rather than hidden.

2. **Ex-ante beta-neutral / risk-controlled spread**
   - weights constructed using only information available at the rebalance timestamp;
   - beta-neutral, inverse-volatility, or another predeclared risk-control method appropriate to the experiment;
   - the method must not be chosen after observing final performance.

3. **Attribution bridge**
   - quantify the difference between raw and risk-controlled returns;
   - attribute material differences to market-beta asymmetry, volatility asymmetry, sector/size exposure, turnover/cost changes, and financing where measurable;
   - do not relabel systematic exposure as alpha decay or alpha creation.

If a risk-controlled counterpart genuinely cannot be constructed for a specific experiment, the omission must be explicitly documented and justified in the experiment record before results are interpreted. Silent omission is not allowed.

### Governing rule

**Risk-controlled portfolios supplement the raw academic spread; they do not replace it. Both views are the default requirement for long-short anomaly/factor tests. Any exception must be explicit, predeclared where possible, and justified.**

## 3. LLM governance is tiered by output type and auditability

A blanket prohibition on modern LLMs touching historical filings is too broad. The relevant distinction is whether the model performs auditable extraction from the as-filed source or generates subjective semantic alpha that may be contaminated by memorized future information.

| LLM application | Risk level | v0.8 governance standard |
|---|---|---|
| **Deterministic / auditable extraction** — debt maturities, customer names, litigation amounts, explicitly disclosed counterparties, table rows, or footnote facts | Lower, but not zero | Allowed on historical filings only when each emitted fact/entity/value is traceable to the original as-filed document and independently verifiable. Exact source spans or equivalent fact grounding must be retained. Unsupported inference is rejected rather than filled in. |
| **Subjective semantic scoring** — sentiment, tone, narrative uncertainty, causal interpretation, management-quality scoring, open-ended risk scoring, or return forecasting | High | Not accepted as historical confirmatory alpha when generated by a model that may contain post-event knowledge. Use a genuinely point-in-time model/corpus where auditable, a fixed deterministic method/lexicon, or prospective real-time evaluation. |

### Additional controls for deterministic extraction

- Preserve the original accession/document hash.
- Preserve the exact source span or structured fact used to support every extracted output where feasible.
- Record model/version, prompt/schema version, extraction time, and deterministic post-validation result.
- Reject or mark `UNVERIFIED` any output that cannot be grounded back to the as-filed source.
- Never permit the extraction stage to add causal, predictive, or outcome-based interpretation unless that task is governed under the higher-risk semantic rules.

### Governing rule

**Historical LLM use is permitted for auditable extraction, not hindsight-contaminated semantic alpha. Auditability and task type—not the mere presence of an LLM—determine the governance tier.**

## 4. Canonical v0.8 interpretation

These three refinements supersede the corresponding overly broad recommendations in the original external audit:

- CPCV does **not** replace sealed chronological or prospective evidence.
- Ex-ante beta/risk control does **not** replace the raw dollar-neutral academic spread; both are reported by default, with any exception explicitly justified.
- Modern LLMs are **not** categorically barred from historical parsing; auditable extraction is allowed, while subjective semantic alpha remains restricted.

They are binding requirements for the v0.8 remediation draft and any implementation built under it.
